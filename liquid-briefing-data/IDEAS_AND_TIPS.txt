The Liquid Briefing: project ideas and tips
MAIN AI Hackathon, Maastricht, 20 to 21 November 2026

Every direction below ends in the same output: a personal audio briefing,
generated from this dataset, for at least two clearly different listeners,
with each claim linked to its source article. They are starting points and
questions, not solutions. How you get there is up to you.

All numbers in this file were counted on the files in this zip
(1,740 articles, as of 19 November 2026).


DIRECTIONS TO EXPLORE

1. The listener who speaks another language
   A Dutch investor and a German one wake up to the same markets. What
   should each of them hear, and in which language? The dataset has news in
   English, German and Dutch.

2. Two people, two mornings
   An FX trader and someone who just wants to know what happened in the
   world have ten minutes each. What makes their briefings different: the
   stories, the depth, the length, the tone?

3. One story, many sources
   When several outlets report the same event, what does the listener need
   to hear once, and what is worth hearing from each source?

4. Numbers you can trust by ear
   A listener cannot scroll back to check a figure. How does your briefing
   make sure every number is right, and how does the listener find out
   where it came from?

5. Cause and reaction
   A central bank speaks, a data release lands, markets move. Can the
   briefing connect what happened with what it meant for the listener's
   money?

6. Every morning, not just once
   Would a newsroom be able to produce this every day, for many listeners,
   at a cost it can carry? What does one briefing cost, and what happens at
   ten times the volume?


TIPS, BY JUDGING CRITERION

"Trust and accuracy: Every figure and claim in the audio links to its source
article, and the numbers are right"
- Every article has a permalink (1,740 of 1,740) and a unique $id. The
  permalink is the link to cite.
- 1,506 of the 1,740 articles contain at least one percentage or dollar
  figure.
- The POSITIVE and NEGATIVE values in categories are not reliable. All 120
  German Invezz articles are tagged NEGATIVE, including German versions of
  articles tagged POSITIVE in English.

"Fit to the listener: Two listeners get clearly different story choice,
depth, length or language"
- Invezz publishes in English, German and Dutch. 119 of 120 German and 119
  of 120 Dutch articles have an English counterpart by the same author,
  published within 15 minutes.
- The outlets differ. ActionForex covers currencies and central banks.
  IBTimes UK covers general news (world, politics, entertainment, crime).
  Invezz covers stocks, crypto and AI. Zacks covers equity and ETF research.
- provider_categories holds each publisher's own sections (for example
  "Stock market", "Crypto", "World", "Fed"). company_tickers is set on only
  30 of 1,740 articles.

"Making sense of the news: Stories grouped, duplicates merged, key facts and
figures extracted"
- The same events are covered by more than one outlet. Between 13 and 18
  November, for example: US producer prices for October (Invezz and
  ActionForex), the shareholder lawsuit against Intel (Invezz and IBTimes
  UK), and Jensen Huang's exit from Nvidia (Invezz, IBTimes UK and Zacks).
- The three Invezz files largely carry the same articles in English, German
  and Dutch.
- Zacks is 1,000 of the 1,740 articles, but only 71 of them are from
  13 November onward, the period the other five feeds cover.

"Scalability and cost: Could run daily on a newsroom's volume, with large
models only where needed"
- One weekday in this dataset is 172 to 190 articles across the six feeds
  (16 to 18 November). The two weekend days had 60 and 42.
- The plain text (text_content) of all 1,740 articles is about 9.0 million
  characters. The HTML version (html_content) is about 12.4 million.
- The presentation asks for your cost for the full dataset and at ten times
  the volume.

"Listening experience: Would you listen to it tomorrow morning?"
- The median article is 3,700 to 4,900 characters long, depending on the
  feed.
- The licence does not allow reading article text out in the audio. Short
  quotes and naming the source are fine.

"Path to product: Who pays for it, and how it fits a newsroom's day"
- Newstex delivers licensed content from many outlets through one feed;
  this dataset is six of them. Think about who in a newsroom would use what
  you build, and when in their day.


GOOD TO KNOW
- category_scores holds a signed number per Newstex category code. It is on
  every article except 3 in Invezz NL.
- thumb_url (an image) is on every article except most of Zacks (326 of
  1,000 have one).
- provider_tickers is empty on every article.
